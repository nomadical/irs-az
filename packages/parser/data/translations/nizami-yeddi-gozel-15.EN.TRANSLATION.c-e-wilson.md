O word-conjoining sorcerer have done! How long, how long will you speak bygone words?

Like to the rose breathe out from your own mouth; enough that your own mouth perfume your work.

E’en at the first my covenant was made — with whom? — with Him whose covenants are true

That what a certain other bard had said — I still drink wine, but he has gone to sleep —

I would not make (such) thought of his my own: ill-doing I'd not make my attribute.

So far as may be, like the vernal breeze, my claim shall not be patching up old things.

Still, to the treasury when one the road — the target one, although the arrows two —

Though in the art of boring pearls (72S) We bound myself not to repeat what has been said,

When repetition I can not avoid, I can produce fine silk from woollen stuff.

Two workers by the alchemy of speech have turned old metal into something new:

That one has made from copper silver pure, and from the silver this one makes pure gold.

Since you’ve seen copper to fine silver turned, be not surprised if silver turns to gold.

The joiner of the parts of this high throne thus adds to it a part correctly joined: —

As soon as Bahram Gur became aware that one, a stranger, had usurped the crown.

He actively prepared for enmity, to seek the crown of the Kayanian kings.

In (this his) quest of universal sway Numan and Muimr gave him (every) help:

Treasure more plentiful than could be named; jewels more numerous than one could bore.

He raised up countless troops, the hostile man was full of fire, hostility had force.

’Twas reckoned, down to Aden, in Yaman, a hundred thousand troopers mustered were:

All clad in steel and iron-strong, to avenge, to bind up demons, and to capture forts.

In heart a lion, each of them, and fit to hold a country by a single sword.

The army of the king set off with speed — the moisture reached the fish, the dust the moon.

The trumpet’s blare, the roll of brazen drum would take all trace of courage from the heart.

The brazen kettle-drum cried out aloud, the drummer (by the noise of it) was stunned.

The plain and mountain from the blare and noise in ferment rose against their lids, the skies.

Troops more than ants and locusts in their count, as hot in rancour as the fire of hell.

They sought the station of the throne (for him), and went from Yaman towards the capital.

The monarch of the world received the news that a young dragon had unclosed its jaws.

That to the earth the heavens had inclined, Canopus had from Yaman raised its head.

A lion had stretched out Lis claws with might to put his foe as ‘‘ gur ” into the “ gur ”.

That he would take the throne and seize the crown — would settle down himself, and lay the dust.

The nobles and the ministers and guards all came together to the royal court.

They met in congress and expressed their views; they spumed all arrogance away (from them).

After reflection they decided thus, that they would write a letter to Bahram.

AH that good sense dictated, that, they wrote, they sowed seed which had (previously) been peeled.

The letter written out they folded it, and made their preparations to depart.

They (all) alighted when they reached (their goal); Fortune asked blessings on the rightful king.

They sought an audience, which was granted them; the chamberlains gave heed to their concerns.

Bahram, the king, allowed them to approach from the (more) distant place (at which they stood).

They went before him with a thousand fears; they bowed down to the ground, and offered thanks.

He who the ball of wisdom bore away, kissing the superscription, gave the note.

After unsealing it the monarch’s scribe (then) read it to the kingdom-taking king.

[From shell to kernel of that beauteous note — the shell was silk, the kernel almond was.

Its outside was adornment of brocade; its inside was an oil-containing lamp].
