The king indulged in pleasure and delights, for he was wearied with the march and fight.

He ordered each one’s business, such as ’twas; then engaged in managing his own.

To his heart’s wish he settled it at ease, wine in hisnd, his foes beneath his feet. '

The story of that master he recalled who formerly had left him that account.

That inner room of the Seven Portraits too, the envy rather of the Seven Climes.

The love those girls of huri form displayed sowed in his heart the seed of love (for them).

His furnace, those seven alchemies at hand, no longer with the seven fusings worked.

The first girl (there) was of Kayanian race; her father had departed from the world.

He asked for her with more than thousand gifts, and e'en through (shining) virtues gained a pearl.

Sending a courier to the khaqan then — partly in amity, in part with threats —

He asked Mm for his daughter with the crown and treasure, tribute for seven years besides.

The khaqan give his daughter, tribute too, a load of “ dinars and a store of gems.

And then he made an inroad into Rum, and poured into that land and country fire.

Through fear the Qaisar acquiesced at once; with deprecation gave his daughter too.

Then sending to the king of Barbary — with gold of Barbary, the crown, and throne.

He gained possession of his daughter (fair) — see how he used astuteness (in his quest)!

Then the straight cypress from that garden borne, he went thence to the realm of Hindustan.

With sense and judgment he demanded (there) the daughter of the Ray, and gained his wish.

His courier went and asked from Khyarazm a lovely girl fit to adorn a feast.

He wrote to Saqlab, and demanded there a beauty as a drop of water bright.

When from the rulers of the Seven Climes he had received seven girls like precious pearls.

He gave his heart exclusively to joy; did justice to gay living and to youth.
